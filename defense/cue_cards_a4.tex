policy we test. Then: Kardex, a carousel brings it; low and hand-reachable, an operator

\sslide{SLIDE 3 \,\textperiodcentered\, SimPy logic}{\(\approx\)85 s}%
{``This is what makes it credible: the model is distribution-driven, not a replay. From four
months of the dispatch log, over 160,000 rows, I fit the arrivals, the kit sizes, the kit
contents per line, and the pick times, then sample from them. Because demand is generated,
not fixed, I can re-slot the materials and re-run the exact same warehouse under any policy,
and the comparison stays fair. The pick-time variability comes straight from the F400 video
study, and all six policies share common random numbers.''}

{\Q{Q1. Trace replay or a real model?}{Distribution-driven, not a replay. I fit
 distributions from the 4-month log and sample, so I can re-slot and re-run any policy.
 Python + SimPy.}
 \Q{Q2. Why discrete-event simulation / SimPy?}{The bottleneck is queueing for shared
 resources and the slot-hold cascade, which static models miss. SimPy makes every resource
 explicit.}
 \Q{Q3. Where do the pick times come from?}{The F400 video study, 2,319 micro-events.
 Lognormal pick times, with the spread taken from the measured CV.}
 \Q{Q4. Kardex timing, no Kardex video?}{Its mean and CV mirror the operator pick,
 disclosed. It cannot bias the result: Kardex routing is identical across all six
 policies.}}
{\Q{Q5. F400 extrapolated to nine families?}{F400 is about 29\% of rows; a $\pm$20\%
 sensitivity sweep keeps Line-aware first under every swing. Per-line studies are future
 work.}
 \Q{Q6. Why batch arrivals, does the rate match?}{The log arrives in bursts: 3,885 batches,
 mean 4.68 kits per batch. The runs land about 400 a day, within 1.1\% of the real 396.}
 \Q{Q7. Why 20 replications, same seeds?}{Seed $42+i$; common random numbers give every
 policy the same demand. The 95\% CIs are narrow and non-overlapping, so 20 is enough.}
 \Q{Q8. Why six policies, not only yours?}{To benchmark fairly: SAP baseline, a heuristic
 control, two ABC variants, travel-distance, and Line-aware. Without references, ``better''
 means nothing.}}

% ════════ PAGE 3 : Q9-16 ════════
\sheet
{\Q{Q9. Chi-square significant, model wrong?}{No. $\chi^2(7)=535$, but Cramer's V is only
 0.22. Significant because the model uses its own routing, not a replay; read the effect
 size.}
 \Q{Q10. No holdout, same data for fit and validation?}{Correct and disclosed: SAP logged
 no waits or RT calls. Grounding: F400 video, daily volume within about 1\%, zero rack
 crossings.}
 \Q{Q11. Aisle congestion not modelled?}{Absolute times are slightly optimistic, but all
 six policies share it symmetrically, so the ranking holds. It would only widen Line-aware's
 lead.}
 \Q{Q12. How real is the SAP baseline?}{An honest SAP-plus-heuristic hybrid: 750 at exact
 SAP bins, 2,872 Kardex, the rest heuristic. Not a pure replay, but our ground truth.}}
{\Q{Q13. Throughput flat, RT only 1.3\%?}{Demand-limited: same arrivals, so about 398 a day
 for all. The 1.3\% RT is picking only, fair across all six; the live total would be
 higher.}
 \Q{Q14. What about the milk-run trains?}{Seven are defined as a transport mode, but the
 operator carries the kit in these runs. Enabling it offloads walking, not the reach-truck
 queue.}
 \Q{Q15. Is the warehouse layout real?}{Reconstructed from the AutoCAD drawing and 11 rack
 PDFs, blind-CAD checked. 3,101 modelled positions reconcile with the 3,203 drawn.}
 \Q{Q16. Can someone reproduce your results?}{Yes: fixed-seed and deterministic; 6 policies
 $\times$ 20 replications = 120 runs, exported for Minitab.}}
